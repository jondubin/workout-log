# K Boges — Health & Fitness OS extract

## Current conclusions

### Direct evidence

- K Boges’ stated aim is sustainable health, physical capability, and an athletic physique—not bodybuilding or powerlifting specialization. His core framing is simple, adaptable, evidence-based calisthenics and nutrition that fit real life. [Source](https://www.kboges.com/)
- He explicitly emphasizes recovery/fatigue management, deloading, retesting, autoregulation, and adjusting training to recovery/performance. [Source](https://www.kboges.com/course)
- His public resources include a first-pull-up program (rings; “as long as it takes”) and a pull-up program for people who can perform 5–12 reps (bar; 3 days/week; 6–12+ weeks). [Source](https://www.kboges.com/resources)

### Interpretation

- The useful “K Boges style” is a principle set, not a fixed branded template: choose basic movements, apply high-quality hard-enough work frequently, distribute fatigue, and adapt volume/effort to recovery.
- The tracker should prioritize adherence, movement quality, proximity to failure, session fatigue, pain, and progression—not only PRs.

## Exercises / protocols worth tracking

### Direct or strongly corroborated

- Core movement categories: one push, one pull, one knee-dominant leg movement; commonly represented by push-ups, pull-ups/rows, and squats/deep knee bends.
- Full-body, high-frequency structure is associated with his “daily full body” material: a community discussion quotes the example of moving from 3 weekly sessions × 4 sets/movement to 6 weekly sessions × 2 sets/movement. This is second-hand, but includes a reply attributed to K Boges. [Discussion](https://www.reddit.com/r/bodyweightfitness/comments/143dtmk/opinions_on_kboges_daily_full_body_training_suggestion/)
- Pull-up specialization: track baseline max reps, 3 training days/week, and program duration of 6–12+ weeks for people starting at 5–12 pull-ups. [Source](https://www.kboges.com/resources)

### Interpretation / tracker defaults

- Let each movement slot support a progression ladder: easier regression → standard version → harder leverage/range/load variation.
- Record each set as `exercise · reps · load/assistance · RIR/RPE · ROM/form · pain`.
- Allow “micro sessions” spread through the day; they fit the low-friction, fatigue-managed intent better than forcing one long workout.
