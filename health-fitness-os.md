# Health & Fitness OS — Working Notes

## Direct evidence

### Current conclusions

- Training style: K Boges–inspired high-frequency basics—accumulate hard sets across the week rather than rely on long, crushing sessions.
- Core weekly volume: roughly **10–20 hard sets per pattern** (push, pull, legs), built up gradually; Kyle’s framework gives a broader **6–20 sets/week** range.
- A hard set: good form and roughly **1–4 reps from max**, without needing to estimate RIR precisely every set.
- Frequency is mainly a way to distribute volume and manage fatigue; weekly recoverable volume matters more than a rigid daily schedule.
- Skipping a day is fine. The relevant question is whether weekly work accumulates without excessive fatigue.
- Main exercises likely: push-ups, pull-ups, and a squat/lunge pattern. Variations are for progression, comfort, and overuse management—not exercise collecting.
- Primary interface principle: simple daily set logging; selected date acts as “today,” with history showing that date and earlier dates.

### Exercises / protocols

- Push: Push-up; diamond, deficit, incline, ring, and weighted variations available.
- Pull: Pull-up; chin-up; wide pull-up; ring row; inverted row.
- Legs: Split squat; Bulgarian split squat; squat; walking lunge; weighted split squat.
- Potential additions:
  - **ATG split squat:** stationary, deep split-squat progression.
  - **ATG lunge:** more dynamic/stepping version; related but distinct from the split squat.
  - Deficit push-ups: potentially useful for chest emphasis if shoulders tolerate the deeper range.
  - Overhead pressing with an empty/light 45 lb bar: user is curious; not yet adopted.

### Specific numbers and progression rules

- Weekly target/orientation: **10–20 hard sets per push, pull, and legs pattern**.
- Wider source framework: **6–20 sets/week** per pattern.
- Typical distribution: **1–3 sets per pattern**, repeated across the week as recovery permits.
- Rep range: roughly **5–35 reps** can work when sets are sufficiently fatiguing; legs may sometimes run higher.
- Rest: framework referenced roughly **1–3 minutes**, or spreading sets across the day.
- Progression options: add reps, improve ROM, slow eccentric, pause in stretch, use a harder variation, add load, or add weekly sets gradually.
- Add volume only when current volume is recoverable and performance/form remain solid; do not force progression through fatigue.

## Interpretation / working assumptions

- A sensible starting structure is **one hard push, one hard pull, and one hard leg set on training days**, then accumulate toward the weekly range. This is an interpretation of the framework, not a mandatory Kyle prescription.
- Five hard sets of one pattern in one day can be valid, but may create enough local/systemic fatigue to reduce the week’s total productive volume. Spreading volume is usually more sustainable.
- For the “big chest” goal, push-up progression—especially deficit push-ups if shoulder-friendly—can be the main chest movement; external load is useful once bodyweight progressions become impractical.
- ATG split squats are likely the cleaner learning choice before dynamic ATG lunges because they are easier to control and load progressively.
- The exercise tracker should prescribe as little as possible: record hard sets and surface weekly totals, while keeping exercise choice flexible.
